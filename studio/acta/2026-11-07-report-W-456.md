**Equivalence.** 11 pool runs against the serial run, 5,213 per-test results (PASS/FAIL lines and TAP ok lines): no status differs in any run. Only differences are the 12 per-file exit markers and one name containing a timing (`elapsedMs=0/1`).
**New flakes:** none in 11 runs (including 2 at pool 24, which oversubscribes the CPU). That bounds a flake rate only to roughly under 25% per run.
**Must stay serial:** none observed. I saw no pool failure and read no fixed port or shared path: tests use mkdtemp fixtures, next-world sets its own HOME, process.chdir is per process, and the scratch repo was clean after every run. The 12 files in 051 are slow within themselves (serial tests of up to 25 s), so splitting them gains nothing past the longest.

**Could not determine:** the hosted CI runner's core count (a pool of 8 on 4 cores may lose the gain); whether a rare flake needs more than 11 runs; the unsplit-051 variant (measured only by reasoning, at least 9.2 min); test names with variable text were matched by a normalising heuristic; lint and other gate steps outside the two scripts were not timed; all runs were on one day.

Numbers favour pool 8: 6.9 min saved, peak CPU 55% and load 15, against 12's 79% and 20 for no further gain. Pool 4 is the light option (4.4 min).
