## Decree

1. **When.** The retro is a step of `next`, after an opus is done; the next
   opus does not start until it is filed. It is never left to memory.
2. **Source.** The retro reads the opus's recorded review verdicts and reds
   (`ci/`), not anyone's recollection. Every finding becomes a lesson or is
   marked "not a lesson" with a reason.
3. **Every lesson names its fix** (an opus, a check rule or a decision);
   the retro files a missing fix as a new opus on the spot.
4. **Small fixes ride with the opus that caught them** when they fall in
   the same files (a test row, a check row) and land before its done.
   Anything else is its own opus.
5. **Severity sets priority**, by rule over recorded facts, not a model:
   high (security, data loss, or a class seen twice) goes to the top of the
   queue; medium (a blocking finding) is ranked normally; low (an advisory)
   goes to the backlog.
6. **High-severity fixes start without a greenlight.** They are greenlit by
   this decision and reported at the next checkpoint, where the Patron may
   halt them.

W-137 carries 1–5; the "untouched backlog" warning in its earlier draft is
dropped from it. W-138 adds cost weighting on top.
