## Intent

D-075 D: a `Brief: light` brief with one behaviour, at most three Files owned and no path W-255 refuses, signed by the architect, goes to branch with no Codex spec round; the Codex build review stays the one gate. D-075 measures cycle minutes from the first spec signature; a spec review round is the largest fixed cost a one-behaviour opus pays before its build.

Today (master c675e04b) the spec rung in `packages/cli/src/next.ts` reads `readSpecReviewer` (line 180): with `bisellium.yml#spec_reviewer` set, as the real studio sets it, every non-past-ready opus gets `reviewedSpec` (line 340), which orders the reviewer after the architect's signature and counts failed reviews toward the two-review split (`failedSpecReviews`, D-061 §4). W-337 and W-348 changed the review and UI rungs, not this choice. A light brief (W-255) is read by one form reader, `briefForm`/`readBriefForm` in `packages/commands/src/brief-admission.ts`, which next's `specSectionsProblem`, ready's and check's admission (`readBriefAdmission`, `admissionLimit`) all read. It refuses a misspelt or repeated marker and security, sandbox, branch-protection and pipeline work (the `RISKY_*` lists, W-255's refused paths), but it admits a light brief of any behaviour count up to `brief_behaviour_limit` and any number of Files owned, and a signed light brief still goes to the reviewer.

After this opus:

1. **"Light" is defined once, in the form reader.** `briefForm` reads a brief as light only when it carries the one real line `Brief: light`, no refused path or family (unchanged), at most one numbered behaviour (zero stays admission's own "numbers no behaviours" problem) and one to three `## Files owned` bullets. A brief that claims light and breaks a rule is `{ light: true, error }`; every reader turns that error into a refusal with its reason: ready and check (`brief.admission`) refuse it, and next's signature gates name the architect's revision with the reason as `why`. A `Behaviour limit exception:` never lifts the one-behaviour rule.
2. **The fast lane is one choice in `deriveNext`, and it fails closed.** With a reviewer configured, next drops the reviewer only when every opus record it reads (the trunk's, and the branch's once `opus/<id>` exists) carries a kind `isNativeOpusKind` accepts other than exact `ui`, and the brief `readBriefForm` reads as light with no error; an absent, non-string, unregistered or mis-cased kind (`UI`) keeps the reviewer: before `opus/<id>` exists the main checkout's brief decides; after it, both the worktree's and the committed branch's must. The spec is then judged by today's no-reviewer reader, `signedSpec`, bound to the brief's blob before the branch (W-293), so next names branch once the architect's passed signature names the current brief. Every other brief and every opus outside the lane keeps today's sequence: the reviewer order, the reviewer model, the two-review split; a brief that claims light and is not is first returned to the architect with its reason by the signature gate.
3. The Codex build review is untouched and stays the one gate.

The fix belongs in the form reader because every path already reads it (one rejecting reader, D-085 D); the lane belongs at the one place the reviewer is chosen. A parallel "fast" reader would let ready and next disagree about what light means.

Threat model: mistakes, not adversaries (D-045). The architect signs; the build review reads the source diff and checks it against the bullets.
