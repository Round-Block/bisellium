# W-162 — spec review before build: next requires one recorded Codex review after the architect’s newest signature

Status: **signed spec, narrowed revision 5** · drafted by Codex (`gpt-5.6-sol`), reviewed, corrected and signed by the architect (`claude-opus-5-5`) under D-027 · 2026-10-07.

This revision applies the Patron’s 2026-10-07 split. It retains only the D-046 spec-review gate. It answers spec-review findings r1 #1–2, r2 #1, r3 #1–2, and n1 #1–2 (the review of revision 4). Findings transferred by the split are listed under **Handed to W-167**.

## Intent

Decree family: spec-review-gate

The decree: **in this studio, `next` names no `branch` until one recorded Codex `gpt-5.6-sol` review has passed after the architect’s newest signature. The review asks only whether the brief’s Input domain names every record it reads with one rejecting function that fails closed, and whether every promise can be guaranteed or states its limit. A blocking finding returns the brief to the architect; the architect’s next signature requires a new review.**

This is D-046 §1 and the enforcement half of §3, under D-027’s order: Codex drafts, the Claude architect reviews and signs, then a different Codex dispatch reviews the signed brief.

The review is another spec-phase verdict written by the existing `bisellium verdict <id> --phase spec`. Architect signatures and Codex reviews therefore share `ci/<id>-spec-<n>.log`, ordered by their positive round number. No new verb, lifecycle state, probatio or log kind is added.

The reviewer is configured by `bisellium.yml#spec_reviewer`. The bisellium studio declares a live `kind: agent` seat on the `codex` harness whose model is exactly `gpt-5.6-sol`. An absent setting preserves the existing ladder for other studios. A malformed or unsuitable setting holds `next` at `spec`; it never disables the gate by default.

With the setting present, `next` reads every matching spec log strictly. The newest passing architect log is the signature. Exactly one valid reviewer verdict may follow it:

- no review orders the reviewer;
- a passed review permits the existing spec commit, landing and then `branch`;
- a failed review orders the architect to revise and sign again;
- an invalid log holds at `spec`;
- any newer architect signature makes every earlier review historical and requires a new review;
- a second reviewer verdict after a failed review, without an intervening architect signature, cannot erase the finding and holds.

Logs at or below the newest valid architect signature are history: only their names are checked. This is the recovery path for a malformed or unwanted log, since `ci/` logs are never edited by hand: the architect signs again at a higher round, and everything older stops counting. A malformed log above the newest valid signature is never skipped; it holds until that new signature exists.

**Both verdicts are bound to the brief’s bytes (answers n1 #2).** W-126 already pins the brief for build verdicts with `# brief: <path> blob:<git blob sha1>`. This opus extends the same header to non-UI spec-phase verdicts: `runVerdict` writes `# brief: briefs/<id>.md blob:<sha1>` from the brief it reads at recording time. A signature or review counts only when its blob equals the blob of the brief in the source being read. If the brief is edited after either verdict, no signature matches. The architect is then ordered to sign again at the next round, and the new signature needs a new review. An edit therefore never reaches `commit`, landing or `branch` unsigned or unreviewed.

**The gate applies until `ready`.** `readSpecReviewer` reads the committed trunk record’s `state`. While it is `greenlit`, the gate applies. Once the merged record is in a `PAST_READY` state (`building`, `verifying`, `review`, `done`), the spec passed its gate when `ready` was recorded on the branch. Step 2 then keeps today’s signature check, so a brief edited by the opus’s own PR does not send a merged opus back to `spec`. The same rule covers opera specced before this opus, W-162 included, whose logs carry no `brief` header.

The verdict outcome must agree with its findings: `passed` has no standing blocking finding, while `failed` has at least one. Thus a mistaken `passed` header beside a blocker cannot unlock `branch`.

**Stated `kind: ui` limit.** The current verdict writer reserves every UI spec-phase verdict for `ui-lead`, and `authoritativeUiInput` (`packages/commands/src/opus-model.ts`) treats the highest-numbered spec log as that UI input. A separate reviewer log therefore cannot yet be recorded without corrupting the UI-input convention. With the setting present, `readSpecReviewer` (new) holds a `kind: ui` opus at `spec` and names this limit. The studio currently has no `kind: ui` opus.

**Stated pre-gate spec-branch limit.** `performSpecCommit` refuses when `spec/<id>` already exists, so a `spec/<id>` branch that carries a signature but no passed review cannot take a review. With the setting present, `next` does not land such a branch. It holds at `spec` and names the branch. Today the only spec branches (`spec/W-085`, `spec/W-140`) belong to opera already `done`, and `next` never reads the spec for those.

**Limits under D-045’s mistakes-not-adversaries threat model.** The seat, harness and exact-model checks catch a misconfigured or wrongly attributed review. The model header remains the recorder’s claim: the repository cannot prove which service actually produced the text. The command can require a findings-shaped record and an outcome consistent with it, but cannot prove that the reviewer reasoned well or considered every promise. Those are review judgments, not mechanically guaranteeable facts. An unreadable `Src` listing is currently indistinguishable from no evidence; that still fails closed before `branch`, although its diagnostic may say the signature or review is missing rather than that the directory is unreadable.

`parseVerdictLog` (new) preserves the strict W-137 grammar: one writer-shaped header block, exactly one `opus` equal to the requested opus, exactly one `## Findings` section, and either `No findings` alone or uniquely numbered blocking/advisory findings. Existing conversion handling remains unchanged for retro.

`readSpecSignature` (new) additionally requires one each of `phase`, `round`, `sella` and `outcome`; `phase: spec`; the filename round; the design magister’s sella; exactly one `brief` header equal to the expected value; exact outcome `passed`; and no standing blocking finding.

`readSpecReview` (new) additionally requires one each of `phase`, `round`, `sella`, `model` and `outcome`; `phase: spec`; the filename round; the configured reviewer sella; exact model `gpt-5.6-sol`; exactly one `brief` header equal to the expected value; and exact outcome `passed` or `failed`. `passed` requires no standing blocker. `failed` requires at least one. Every numbered finding must carry the writer’s `check: <value>` shape.

With a reviewer configured, `specEvidence` reads all entries beginning `<id>-spec-` in the source it is given (the trunk, `spec/<id>` or the main checkout). A name outside `<id>-spec-<n>.log`, where `n` is a positive integer without a leading zero, is invalid. Every matching log is parsed before it is classified. The newest valid architect signature anchors the sequence. Every log above it must be a valid reviewer verdict, and at most one may follow it. Another sella, an unreadable log or malformed evidence above the anchor is invalid, not ignored. Logs at or below the anchor are history. With no valid anchor (none signed, or every signature names another blob), the architect is ordered at the highest round plus one. The blob is the git blob sha1 of the brief’s text as the source returns it, encoded as UTF-8.

Its result distinguishes: missing signature, missing review, failed review, invalid evidence, or ready. Only ready permits `performSpecCommit`, landing or `branch`. Without the setting, `specEvidence` preserves today’s behaviour.

The reviewer order is:

```text
dispatch <sella> (<model>) to review the signed spec once, on two questions only (D-046):
1. does its Input domain name every record the opus reads, with one rejecting function that fails closed?
2. can every promise be guaranteed, or does it state its limit?
Record findings under ## Findings, each ending "check: …", or write "No findings".
Record with: bisellium verdict <id> --round <n> --sella <sella> --model gpt-5.6-sol
  --outcome <passed|failed> --phase spec --from <file> --studio <studio>
```

## Behaviours to test

Each behaviour is a `node:test` in `packages/cli/src/next.test.ts`, named `W-162-b<n> behaviour <n>: …` and selectable with `--test-name-pattern=W-162-b<n>`. Tests use copied fixtures or scratch repositories and never write `studio/` or `examples/`.

1. **A valid reviewer setting sends a signed spec to the reviewer, while bad settings and UI opera hold.**
   - A working-tree architect signature at round 1 with no review, and none on the trunk, names a spec-reviewer order at round 2, model `gpt-5.6-sol`, with the brief and signature log as inputs.
   - The command contains the two D-046 questions and the exact verdict command.
   - An absent key preserves the existing commit path.
   - `null`, an unknown or retired seat, the design magister, a non-agent seat, a non-Codex harness, a missing model, and any model other than `gpt-5.6-sol` hold at `spec`.
   - A `kind: ui` opus holds and names the verdict-writer/UI-input limit.

   **Genuine red:** today the working-tree architect signature names the existing `commit` act instead of a reviewer order; the test fails on its `role: spec-reviewer` assertion.

2. **Architect signatures and review verdicts are strict, and their outcomes agree with their findings.**
   - Malformed filenames, missing or duplicate required headers, a missing Findings section, another sella, and a round or phase mismatch hold.
   - A review with a missing or wrong model holds.
   - `bisellium verdict --phase spec` on a non-UI opus writes `# brief: briefs/<id>.md blob:<sha1>`, and refuses with exit 2 when the brief is unreadable.
   - Editing the brief after the signature orders the architect at the next round instead of the reviewer.
   - `passed` beside a blocking finding and `failed` beside `No findings` hold.
   - A malformed newest architect signature is not skipped in favour of an older signature.
   - Existing `retro --opus` parser tests remain green after the parser extraction.

   **Genuine red:** after behaviour 1, a brief edited after its signature still gets the reviewer order rather than the architect; the test fails on `role: architect`. A hand-written reviewer log with `outcome: passed` beside a blocking finding likewise still gets the reviewer order rather than the hold.

3. **Only a passed review after the newest architect signature unlocks commit, landing and branch.**
   - Signature 1 plus passed review 2 in the working tree names `commit`.
   - Performing it commits exactly the brief and both validated spec logs on `spec/<id>`.
   - The accepted spec branch names landing; the landed trunk names `branch`.
   - A `spec/<id>` branch carrying only a signature holds at `spec` and is not landed.
   - The `ready` step names the design magister, not the reviewer, as the spec gate’s sella.
   - Editing the brief after a passed review orders the architect and never names `commit` or `branch`.
   - A trunk record in a `PAST_READY` state with pre-gate logs (no `brief` header, no review) goes past `spec` as today.
   - Signature 1, passed review 2, signature 3 orders a new review at round 4 and never names `branch`.
   - The reviewer log’s model must equal `gpt-5.6-sol`, independently of the sella header.

   **Genuine red:** after behaviour 2, a valid passed review remains classified only as evidence and does not unlock the existing commit act; the test fails on `act: commit`.

4. **A failed review returns to the architect, and only a newer signature can reopen review.**
   - Signature 1 plus failed review 2 orders the architect at round 3, with the brief and failed review as inputs.
   - Signature 3 then orders the reviewer at round 4, with `resume: false`; the architect’s order at round 3 is held by `gateDispatch` until the record carries a `traditio`.
   - Passed review 4 permits the commit.
   - A second reviewer verdict after failed review 2, without an architect signature between them, holds and cannot erase the finding.
   - A malformed log at round 2 holds; a valid signature at round 3 makes it history and orders the reviewer at round 4.

   **Genuine red:** after behaviour 3, a valid failed review is held as non-passing evidence rather than ordering the architect; the test fails on `role: architect`.

## Out of scope

- Build-review round counting, a three-round stop, security-round treatment, Patron round rulings and class/boundary fix instructions. These belong to W-167.
- Changing the UI verdict format or `authoritativeUiInput`. UI opera wait at `spec` under the setting.
- Proving cryptographically that Codex produced the review, or mechanically judging whether its reasoning is complete.
- A new probatio, lifecycle gate, verdict verb or check rule for the setting.
- Changing retro’s accepted verdict grammar or conclusions.
- Rewriting historical spec logs. The new reader matters only when `next` evaluates an opus under the configured gate.

## Defaults for the Patron to veto

1. The rule is enabled by one studio setting. Studios without it keep today’s ladder.
2. The reviewer is a separate live Codex seat using exactly `gpt-5.6-sol`; another Codex model is not close enough.
3. A newer architect signature always needs a newer review, even when an earlier review passed.
4. A failed review cannot be replaced by another reviewer pass. The architect must sign a revision first.
5. The existing verdict command records the review. There is no second spec gate in the opus record.
6. Screen-design opera wait at the spec step until their special verdict format can distinguish UI input from this review.
7. The gate applies while the trunk record is `greenlit`. A merged opus past `ready` is not sent back to `spec` by its own brief edits or by pre-gate logs.
8. Both spec verdicts carry W-126’s brief blob, and an edited brief needs a new signature and a new review.
