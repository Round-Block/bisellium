## Intent

W-268 part 1 (PR #626, 2026-10-20) built the wall from the tree: in an officina with `step_tickets: required`, the officina check blocks `ladder.ticket` at a covered verdict or red log of an open opus that its own ticket does not justify, and the merge queue's required `officina` job runs that check. Two things W-233 named are still unjudged.

**The record's tickets: entries.** The ready, review and done verbs each append their claimed ticket line to the record's `tickets:` list (W-233). Nothing checks an entry once written: a hand-edited entry, one copied from another opus, or one naming the wrong action or round reaches master. After this part, `ladder.ticket` also judges every entry of every record's `tickets:` list, whatever the record's state. There is no history to spare: an entry exists only since the switch (2026-10-14), and a done record is judged too, since the done PR is the one that writes the done entry. An entry is judged alone: a string that `parseTicketLine` accepts, naming the record's own opus, the action ready, review or done, round 0 for ready and done, and for review a round n whose named log `ci/<opus>-review-<n>.log` has unique `# opus:`, `# phase:` and `# round:` headers equal to the record id, build and n (the review verb takes its round from that evidence file's name, `lifecycle.ts` W-233/W-334, so the file's own header must agree with it: revision 3). An entry is not merged into part 1's per-opus log timeline: the chain issues a review ticket at the same instant as the verdict-build ticket it follows (W-354's review entry and its review log's ticket are both `2026-10-21T06:28:40.731Z`), so the log order and the entry order are different sequences.

**The record sequences (W-183's late verdict).** W-183's spec round 8 (`2026-10-11T05:47:59.155Z`) was written after its round 9 (`05:46:34.248Z`). Part 1 does not catch that shape even with tickets: it orders a log by time and asks only that its ticket follows the previous log, so two rounds whose tickets each bind their own log pass in either round order. After this part, `ladder.order` blocks a judged verdict log (part 1's judged set, so the same migration boundary: an open opus, from the officina's first ticket on) whose round repeats, or whose `# at:` is not after, the judged log of the round below it in its opus and phase; and it blocks a record's tickets: entry whose `at` is not after the entry before it (the verbs append one entry per write). W-183 itself (2026-10-11, before the first ticket at `2026-10-14T22:48:53.331Z`) is history and not judged.

**Where it lives.** Both rules extend part 1's one reader, `checkLadderTicket` in `packages/cli/src/rules/ladder-ticket.ts`: the record read moves before the no-ticket-yet return and is read once, the entries are judged from it, and the round order is judged from the logs part 1 already reads. No parallel reader, no new module, no wiring: `check.ts` already calls the rule, `package.json` already runs its test file, and the family `ladder` is already labelled on the Officina screen ("Step tickets"). Nothing here touches `packages/cli/src/next.ts` (W-305's lane).

**Level: block**, as part 1: a warning does not stop the queue. The census (`census.out`) shows master passes: 47 records hold 170 entries, every one canonical, naming its own opus, a record action and its round, each review round with its build verdict log, each after the entry before it; the only round inversion in the whole officina is W-183's, before the boundary. The probes (`probes.out`) find no `ladder.*` finding over master's officina, the main checkout's (its uncommitted W-350 verify files included) or W-340's worktree officina.

Promise record-entries: in an officina whose `bisellium.yml` sets `step_tickets: required`, the officina check blocks `ladder.ticket` at a record, in any state, once for a `tickets:` value that is not a list and once for each entry that is not a string `parseTicketLine` accepts, names another opus, names an action other than ready, review or done, names a round other than 0 for ready or done, or for review names a round n whose log `ci/<opus>-review-<n>.log` is absent or lacks unique `# opus:`, `# phase:` and `# round:` headers equal to the record id, build and n; one finding per entry, the first reason found — limit: the named log's other headers and body (its ticket, outcome, time and findings) are not revalidated here; an entry is judged alone, so whether a review ticket was issued after its evidence log, whether a done entry sits in a done record, how many ready entries a record holds, and an entry's step, actor, studio, head and brief are not judged; a review entry for round 0 passes when a build verdict log named `review-0` exists; a record that cannot be read or parsed is the record rules' (`opus.parse`) and its entries are not judged.

Promise round-order: in such an officina, the officina check blocks `ladder.order` at each judged verdict log, a spec or build log of an open opus at or after the officina's first ticket with an exact UTC `# at:` and a round (part 1's judged set), whose round equals, or whose `# at:` is not after, that of the judged log with the next lower round in its opus and phase; one finding per log — limit: only judged logs are compared, so a pair with a log before the first ticket (W-183's rounds 8 and 9), a log of a done, halted or backlog opus, or a log without an exact time or a round is not ordered; red logs, handoff-phase verdicts and the record's `amendments:` are not ordered.

Promise entry-order: in such an officina, the officina check blocks `ladder.order` at a record, in any state, for each `tickets:` entry that `parseTicketLine` accepts whose `at` is not after the `at` of the previous entry it accepts — limit: an entry it refuses is `ladder.ticket`'s and skipped by the order; the order is the list's and is not compared with the logs' (the chain shares one instant across tickets, W-354).

Promise never-throws: the extended rule still reads only the checked tree and never throws: a `tickets:` value of any YAML shape is one finding or a list judged entry by entry — limit: the reads are part 1's, unchanged (`readContainedRegularFile`, `listMd`, `parseFrontMatter`), a stated limit.

Threat model: mistakes, not adversaries (D-045), as W-233 and part 1: a ticket line can be forged in a file; these rules catch records that disagree with themselves, not every lie.

## Interfaces

The prototype is six commits on master fa53e279 (`prototype-r3.mbox`): for each behaviour in order, `test(W-358): row b<k>, red` then `feat(W-358): behaviour <k>`. `build-commits-r3.sh` rebuilds them in `prototype-repo-r3` from `apply-test-r3.py <checkout> <k>` (rows b1 to b<k>) and `apply-src-r3.py <checkout> <k>` (stages 1 to k, each an exact-once text replacement). The build reproduces them; a hunk that no longer applies after a rebase is redone by hand to the same text.

1. **The entries** (behaviour 1). `entryFindings(where, id, value, logs): Finding[]`, called once per record that parses to a mapping, with `where` the record's contained read path `opera/<file>`. Absent `tickets:` is `[]`; a value that is not a list is one block "`<id>`'s tickets: is not a list of step ticket lines". Each entry, numbered from 1, is one block at most, the first that applies: "entry `<n>` is not a step ticket line (`<parseTicketLine's error>`)" (a non-string reads as the error "not a string"); "entry `<n>` names `<opus>`, want `<id>`"; "entry `<n>` is a `<action>` ticket; a record's tickets: holds only ready, review and done"; "entry `<n>` (`<action>`) names round `<r>`, want round 0"; "entry `<n>` (review) names round `<r>`, but ci/`<id>`-review-`<r>`.log is not `<id>`'s build verdict log for round `<r>`", the last read from the logs part 1 lists: a covered log at that name whose unique `# opus:` is the record id, whose unique `# phase:` is build and whose unique `# round:` reads as `<r>` (part 1's header reader keeps every value of a repeated key, and a value used singly is read only when it appears once, so a repeated or missing header never agrees). Every message starts "`<id>`'s tickets: ". The records are read once, before the no-ticket-yet return, so the entries are judged even before any log carries a ticket; part 1's open set is derived from the same read.
2. **The round order** (behaviour 2). After part 1's log loop: the judged logs (part 1's `covered`) that are verdict logs with a round and an exact time, grouped by opus and action, sorted by round (a tie by path). For each log after the first in its group, against the one before it: an equal round is one block "`<opus>`'s `<phase>` round `<r>` is recorded again after `<path>`: one log per round"; otherwise a time not after it is one block "`<opus>`'s `<phase>` round `<r>` (`<at>`) is not after round `<p>`'s log `<path>` (`<at>`): rounds are recorded in order". `ids.ts` gains `"ladder.order"`; the id is written as `rule: "ladder.order"` so the drift guard in `rules/lex.test.ts` harvests it.
3. **The entry order** (behaviour 3). Inside `entryFindings`, after each entry's own check: an entry `parseTicketLine` accepts whose `at` is not after the previous accepted entry's is one block `ladder.order` "`<id>`'s tickets: entry `<n>` (`<action>`, `<at>`) is not after entry `<m>` (`<action>`, `<at>`): entries are appended in time order". The times are read through part 1's `exact` (`utcTimestampProblem`, then `instant`; no raw date parse).

Limits, stated:

- Unchanged code the promises lean on is a stated limit, not a guarantee (D-076 E): part 1's `checkLadderTicket` (its setting read, log listing, header reader, boundary, open set and per-log checks), `parseTicketLine`, `ticketLine` and `readStepTickets` (`ticket.ts`), `utcTimestampProblem` and `readContainedRegularFile` (`opus-model.ts`), `instant`, `listMd`, `parseFrontMatter`, `checkStudio`'s rule-module block, and the writers that append entries and stamp `# at:` (`lifecycle.ts`'s ready, review and done; `verdict.ts`).
- The judgement is the checked tree's: on a branch it judges the branch's officina, in the merge queue the group's.
- The rule reads one more field per record it already reads; no new file is read.

### Input domain

The rule blocks; where it judges, a rejection fails closed: an entry that cannot be read as a ticket blocks, and a verdict log or entry out of order blocks. An input outside its judgement (a record that does not parse, a log part 1 does not judge, an officina without the setting) is no finding; that boundary is the promises' limit.
