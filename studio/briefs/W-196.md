## Intent

Decree family: brief-form-admission

D-056 makes form advisory in the spec review and moves the form checks into brief admission. Three readers are added to the one admission predicate (`readBriefAdmission`, shared through `briefAdmissionProblems`):

1. **Read enumeration.** A brief cites the kept script its drafting ran and the script's output, on one line `Read enumeration: <script> -> <output>`, both in backticks, or says `Read enumeration: none: <reason>`. Admission checks the citation, not the files.
2. **Absolute promises.** From `## Intent` up to `## Out of scope`, with inline code removed, the phrases `always`, `in every case`, `in all cases`, `exactly as before`, `exactly as on master` and `exactly as today` (whole words, any case) appear only on a promise line, `Promise <slug>: <claim> — limit: <what does not hold>`. Each slug and each claim appears once. Ordinary prose and tables keep `every`, `never` and the rest: their substance stays the review's (D-046 question b).
3. **D-050 rejector forms.** A `Rejected by` cell opens with a backticked function name, as today, or with `runner: <job>` (a required job's step failure), `exit: <call>` (a reused call judged by its exit), each backticked and followed by text, or with `limit: <text>`.

`specOrder` applies the same predicate to the signed brief before it derives the spec reviewer's order (step 2 of the ladder); what `next` then prints is promise `next-review-gate`'s, under its E1–E13 limit (Interfaces). D-056 §3 says "before signing"; the verdict writer cannot call the predicate, because `lifecycle.ts` imports `verdict.ts` and the reverse import would be a cycle. So the gate is the first step after the signature: `next` names no reviewer for an inadmissible brief (under every E-step too, since each holds or prints without one), and when the promise's conditions hold the fix costs one re-signature, not a review round (Defaults 5). This extends what exists (D-042): no runner, manifest key or repository script is added. D-053 removed the spec-round hold, so none is added.

Promise done-exempt: no admission caller reads a done opus's brief — limit: a halted, greenlit or backlog opus's brief written before W-196 is read when it reaches `ready`, `check` or the review order, and is refused until revised (Defaults 3).

## Interfaces

`readBriefAdmission(briefText, limit)` and `briefAdmissionProblems(root, manifest, opusId, briefText, limit)` keep their signatures, and every existing problem keeps its text, except the rejector message, and its place. New problems come after the Input domain's, citation first:

- ``cites no read enumeration; add one line "Read enumeration: `<kept script>` -> `<its output>`", or "Read enumeration: none: <reason>"``
- `has <n> read enumeration lines; one only`, and `read enumeration line "<line>" is not "…" or "…"`
- `line <n> says "<phrase>" outside a promise line; declare it once as "Promise <slug>: <claim> — limit: <what does not hold>"`
- `line <n>: a promise line is "…"`, `line <n>: promise "<slug>" is declared twice; state each promise once`, `line <n>: promise "<slug>" repeats an earlier promise's claim; state each promise once`

The prototype's code is `prototype.mjs --source` (`reads.txt` §2); the builder may restyle it, not change what it admits.

A read-enumeration attempt is a real line (outside fences and comments, as every admission reader) whose trimmed text matches `/^read[\s_-]*enumeration\s*:/i`, the pattern `readRedOrder` uses for its line. A promise attempt is a trimmed line, after an optional `- `, `* ` or `N. ` marker, matching `Promise <word>:`. A claim is compared lower-cased, with whitespace collapsed and trailing `.,;` dropped. Line numbers are the brief's own, 1-based.

`specOrder` derives, at a signed brief awaiting review (gate `review`), where `bisellium.yml` declares `brief_behaviour_limit` (what `next` prints is promise `next-review-gate`'s, below):
