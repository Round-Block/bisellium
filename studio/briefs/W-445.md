## Intent

A brief cites evidence for what it says, and a revision moves the evidence on while a line keeps citing the old file. The four reviews this class came from show the shapes it takes:

- **W-442 spec r4** (`ci/W-442-spec-4.log` #1, the one blocking finding): a revised line quoted the renamed assertion "the today rule is offset beyond the trailing edge" and cited the bare name `kill-K1-policy.out`. Under the brief's evidence root that name is the round-1 file, which still prints the old message; the rerun lived only under `W-442-build/fix1/`. The architect had signed that brief at spec round 3 (`ci/W-442-spec-3.log`). The quoted text and the file are both on the line, so this is decidable.
- **W-361 spec r5** (#2) and **W-366 spec r2** (#3): a sentence's meaning disagreed with a newer cited output (the browser rows "could not run", yet the output shows them run; a screenshot row "flips", yet the output shows it skipped). The disagreement is in what the words mean, not in quoted text.
- **W-408 spec r2** (#5): the title kept pre-rescope wording. No evidence is cited at all.
- Earlier, L-141 (W-209): cited line numbers drifted after another opus merged.

**What "holds the claim" means here, and why.** A cited file holds a claim when the claim's quoted text is in it: anywhere in the file, or on line n for a `<file>:<n>` citation. Presence of quoted text is the only relation between a sentence and a file that a program can decide without judging meaning, and it is the relation the W-442 shape breaks. It is also what briefs already quote: a test row's name and an assertion message, both of which Node's TAP reporter prints verbatim. The form read is the one briefs already use, a quoted claim followed by a parenthesis that opens with the backticked citation, `"<claim>" (`<file>`, …)`. A bare name is resolved under the brief's own evidence roots, its backticked absolute or `~/` directories, as a reader of the brief would resolve it.

**Fail-closed, and the form narrowed to keep it honest (revision 2).** Spec review round 2 found revision 1 fail-open: a relative name under no root was skipped, so a typo in the root or the name signed with its claim unchecked. Revision 2 refuses every recognised citation that does not name exactly one readable file, as the reviewer's row asks. Applied to every parenthesis, that refuses 21 claims in 13 of master's 226 briefs (`census-strict.out`), none of them stale: the parenthesis opens with a symbol (`writeGates`, `classBlocker`), a source or test file (`next.ts`, `next-branch.test.ts`, `.github/CODEOWNERS`), an error code (`ERR_ASSERTION`), a line reference (`:719`) or a glob, each naming code beside the quote, not an evidence file. So a claim is recognised only when its parenthesis opens with an evidence citation: an absolute or `~/` path, or a name ending in `.out` or `.log`, the outputs briefs cite as evidence. On master that leaves 25 claims in the 226 briefs; the check refuses none of them, refuses W-442's signed round-3 brief at exactly the line the reviewer found and admits round 5's (`census.out`), and refuses neither brief in flight (`census-inflight.out`). Every recognised claim is then checked or refused, and only a directory citation (ending in `/`) is left unchecked, by name. The two shapes about meaning, the uncited title and arbitrary prose are stated limits (Promise refuse, Limits).

**Where.** At the signer's spec verdict, beside W-306's promise ledger in `packages/commands/src/verdict.ts`: the one admission point that already reads evidence files (`evidenceText`, the reader of W-381's kills, and its 4 MB cap `LOG_MAX_BYTES`), and the one the architect passes at every signed revision, where W-442's stale line would have been refused at round 3 instead of found at round 4. ## Interfaces

**`evidenceClaims(briefText: string): EvidenceClaim[]`** (exported from `brief-admission.ts`): over `realLines`, every match, in brief order, of a quoted claim, straight `"…"` or curly `“…”`, nonblank, on one line, whose opening quote does not follow a letter, mark, digit, `_` or another quote, followed after optional whitespace by `(`, optional whitespace and a backticked span that is an evidence citation (`EVIDENCE_CITE`: the span, its `:<n>` split off, opens with `/` or `~/` or ends in `.out` or `.log`); a match whose span is anything else is no claim. Each is `{ line, claim, cite, path, at? }`: `cite` the span; `at` a `:<n>` suffix of the span (a positive integer of at most 15 digits), split off `path`.

**`evidenceRoots(briefText: string): string[]`**: every backticked span on a real line that is an absolute or `~/` path ending in `/`, each once, in brief order.

**`evidenceClaimProblems(brief: string): string[]`** (exported from `verdict.ts`): fail-closed: one problem per declared root whose `~/` does not expand to an absolute path, and one per claim whose citation does not name exactly one readable regular file holding it. A `path` ending in `/` is not checked. An absolute or `~/` path is read by `evidenceText` (a readable regular file of at most `LOG_MAX_BYTES`). A relative path matching `RELATIVE_CITE` (slash-separated segments of word characters, `.`, `+` and `-`, none `.` or `..`) is read under the one root where `statSync` finds a regular file; under none or under two or more it is a problem. Any other path is a problem. The file, or its line `at`, holds the claim when `holdsClaim` finds the claim's pieces between its gaps (an `<angle-bracketed placeholder>`, `…` or `...`) in it in order, whitespace runs squeezed to one space.

**`verdict --phase spec`**, by the signer (the design magister, or a `kind: ui` opus's `ui_spec_signer`): after the promise ledger, each problem prints as `<id>: evidence citation: brief:<n> cites `<cite>` for "<claim>", and <file> does not hold it: cite the evidence that does, or correct the claim` (or `… and line <k> of <file> does not hold it: …`, `… and it names no regular file under the evidence roots (<roots>): cite it by its absolute path, or correct the name or the root`, with `the brief declares no evidence root (…)` when there is none, `… and it names a file under <k> evidence roots (<roots>): cite it by its absolute path`, `… and it is not a path the check reads (…): cite the evidence file by such a path`, `… and <file> is not a readable regular file`; and `<id>: evidence citation: evidence root `<root>` does not expand to an absolute path`); the ledger's problems now print as `<id>: promise ledger: <problem>`, their text unchanged. Any problem: exit 2, no log written.

Promise claims: brief admission reads every quoted claim of the form `"<claim>" (`<cite>`` on a real line whose cited span is an evidence citation (absolute, `~/`, or ending in `.out` or `.log`), with its line, its cited span and any `:<n>` line, and no quote glued to a word, no claim without the parenthesis, no claim citing a symbol or a source file, and no fenced or commented claim. Pinned by `W-445-b1` "the claims, in brief order" — limit: a claim in any other form (the citation before the quote, single quotes, a claim spanning lines, a parenthesis whose first span is not the evidence) is not read, an evidence file under any other name (a relative `.md`, `.txt` or `.patch`) is not recognised unless cited by its absolute path, and only the parenthesis's first span is the citation.

Promise roots: the evidence roots are the brief's backticked absolute or `~/` directories ending in `/`, each once. Pinned by `W-445-b1` "the absolute and ~/ directory spans, each once" — limit: a folder named any other way (relative, unquoted prose, no trailing slash) is not a root, so a bare name under it is not read.

Promise refuse: the signer's spec signature is refused, exit 2 and no log, when a cited file it reads does not hold the quoted claim, or the cited line does not, or the pieces around a gap are out of order. Pinned by `W-445-b2` "a bare citation whose file does not hold the claim" and pinned by `W-445-b2` "a cited line that does not hold it" and pinned by `W-445-b2` "pieces out of order" — limit: "holds" is textual presence; whether the claim is true of what the brief says it shows (W-361's and W-366's shapes), an uncited stale sentence (W-408's), an escaped form of the text (a TAP-escaped `#`, a YAML-doubled quote) and the evidence's freshness beyond its text are not judged, and stay the spec review's.

Promise resolve: every recognised citation but a directory names exactly one readable regular file, or the signature is refused: a relative citation is read under the one root holding a regular file by that name and refused under none (a typo in the name or the root, or no root declared) or two or more; an absolute or `~/` citation that is not a readable regular file is refused; a citation that is no readable path is refused; a `~/` root that does not expand to an absolute path is refused. Pinned by `W-445-b2` "a bare citation under two roots" and pinned by `W-445-b2` "an absolute citation of no file" and pinned by `W-445-b2` "a relative citation under no root" and pinned by `W-445-b2` "a typo in the declared root" and pinned by `W-445-b2` "a ~/ root that does not expand to an absolute path" and pinned by `W-445-b2` "an output citation that is not a readable path" — limit: a directory citation (ending in `/`) is not checked, and a root that names a real but wrong folder holding a file by the same name is read there.

Promise seat: only the signer's spec verdict runs the check; another seat's spec verdict, a build or handoff verdict, `check`, `ready` and `next` are unchanged. Pinned by `W-445-b2` "another seat's spec verdict is not judged" — limit: a brief edited after it was signed is not re-read until its next signature, and `next`'s admission (W-196) does not run this check.

Limits, stated:

- Unchanged code the promises lean on is a stated limit, not a guarantee (D-076 E): `realLines`, `evidenceText`, `LOG_MAX_BYTES`, `readDesignMagister`, `readUiSpecPairing`, `promiseLedgerProblems` and the rest of `runVerdict`.
- The evidence files are read when the signature is recorded and are not fingerprinted; a file changed afterwards is not seen, as the ledger is not (W-306).
- A gap (`<…>`, `…`, `...`) stands for any text, so a claim of only gaps holds anywhere.
