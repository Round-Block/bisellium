# W-120 — a fired kill clause is never invoked afterwards

Status: **signed spec, revision 1** · architect sella (claude-opus-5-5) · 2026-10-06.
The spec gate is written by `bisellium verdict W-120 --phase spec`, not by this text.

## Intent

Decree family: fired-clause

The decree: **once a decision's kill clause has fired, any record that
invokes that decision afterwards is a blocking check finding. A fired
clause is never a retroactive excuse.**

P-014 is the ruling. The Patron wrote, 2026-10-01: "Build the check: a
kill or waiver clause invoked after its triggering event is a blocking
finding, never a retroactive excuse." P-020 (cascade 43) was closed into
this opus. The class `process×insurance-clause-fired` has three lessons:

- **L-020, cascade 6.** W-021's insurance clause had been adopted before
  the build. It fired, and it was honoured as written (P-004). The Patron
  wrote: "The insurance clause worked exactly as decreed." This is the good
  case, and the rule must not touch it.
- **L-041, cascade 34.** D-024 waived W-071's red order. Its second kill
  arm says the waiver dies when "a second waiver of this class is
  requested". W-064 asked for that second waiver (P-013). The censor
  refused to read D-024 as cover, and the Patron agreed: "D-024 is not
  extended; its kill arm stands." This is the case the rule exists for.
  Today it was caught by one reviewer's reading, and nothing would have
  failed if the reviewer had missed it.
- **L-061, cascade 43.** A test census cap fired and was routed around in
  `run.test.ts` (W-132 review 1, F1). No decision was cited, so this rule
  cannot see it. That shape belongs to its own class (see Out of scope).

**Measured at master `b822418`.** The script is
`~/.bisellium-evidence/W-120-spec/measure.mjs`, and its output is
`measure.out` beside it.

- 37 decisions. None records that its kill clause has fired. `kill_when`
  is prose, and no field says when it fired.
- 14 recorded invocations, all `halted_by` (D-017 ×2, D-019, D-033 ×3,
  D-038 ×8). There are 0 gate waivers (`waived_by`).
- The record says, in prose, that three kill clauses have fired:
  - D-024, per P-013 and P-012;
  - D-025, when W-079's cascade closed, and D-028 was the fresh decree it
    asked for;
  - D-038, when W-152 put the milestone records into data.

  Not one of the 14 invocations comes at or after its decision's firing:
  the 8 D-038 halts are at 2026-10-05T22:48Z, before W-152 went done. So
  the rule flags **0 records today**, and still 0 once those three
  firings are recorded. No waiver and no migration are needed.

So the missing piece is a field, not a judgment. A fired kill clause gets
a time and a cited record on the decision itself. The check then compares
every time-stamped invocation against it. The check does not try to read
`kill_when` prose, and anything not recorded stays silent.

## Interfaces

```yaml
# decisions/D-nnn.md — two optional keys, written by hand like the rest of a decision
killed_at: 2026-09-25T07:22:38.951Z   # when its kill_when was observed to have fired (YAML date or ISO string)
killed_by: petitiones/P-013.md        # the record that observed it; officina-relative
```

These are `decision.shape` (block) findings, each at `decisions/<id>.md`:

- one of the two keys is present without the other;
- `killed_at` is not a date;
- `killed_at` is earlier than the decision's own `at`;
- `killed_by` does not open through `readContainedRegularFile(root,
  killed_by, <first component>)`. The first component must be one of
  `acta`, `ci`, `decisions`, `lessons`, `opera` or `petitiones`. This is
  the shared containment helper (engineering lex §2), never a raw `join`.

A decision with neither key yields nothing, as all 37 do today.

// decision.invoked_after_kill (block), where: opera/<id>.md. The two invocations judged:

## Out of scope

Each is a follow-on for the producer to file, or someone else's step.

- **The verbs refusing a killed decision.** These are `halt`, `waive`,
  `done`'s human-gate branch (`patronDecisionProblem`) and a brief's
  "Behaviour limit exception:" (`briefAdmissionProblems`). They would fail
  at write time. Until then, the next `check` blocks what they wrote.
- **A `kill` verb** that writes `killed_at` and `killed_by`. No `decide`
  verb exists either, and decisions stay hand-written.
- **A killed decision stops addressing a lesson class.** Today
  `lesson.addressed_by` and `lesson.recurrent` would still count it.
- **Reading `kill_when` prose** to detect a firing. Only a recorded firing
  counts.
- **An invocation dated before its decision's `at`.** The 3 D-033 halts
  at 13:22Z cite a decision hand-typed as 14:30Z. Both landed in one
  commit (044bc88), so a rule on hand-typed times would flag noise.
- **Invocations in prose.** These are verdict logs and `traditio.next`.
  W-071's "passed by D-024's recorded waiver" predates the kill.
- **Cascade 43's shape**, a check ratchet routed around without citing
  any clause. It is `tests×wall-clock-sleep-for-a-missing-seam`'s own
  class (L-035, L-050).
- **Recording the three known firings on the real officina** (D-024,
  D-025, D-038). After merge, this is the producer's record-only PR. So
  is naming `decision.invoked_after_kill` in the class lessons'
  `addressed_by`. A lex amendment adding a `(check: …)` marker is the
  Patron's.

## Defaults for the Patron to veto

Each ships as stated and can be overturned at the checkpoint.

1. **How a fired kill is recorded.** By hand, on the decision: the time it
   fired and the record that saw it fire. The check does not guess from
   the kill clause's wording.
2. **What counts as invoking a decision.** A halt that names it, and a gate
   waiver that names it. These are the two records that carry both a
   decision and a time. Brief exceptions and lessons come later.
3. **The past stands.** Anything that cited the decision before it was
   killed is never flagged. Anything at or after that moment is flagged,
   and the same instant counts as after.
4. **No time means flagged.** A halt or waiver that cites a killed decision
   but has no readable time is treated as coming after the kill.
5. **Finished and halted tasks are judged too.** Only citations after the
   kill can be flagged, so history before it is untouched.
6. **The check only, for now.** The halt and waive commands can still
   write such a citation, and the next check blocks it. Making the
   commands refuse is a follow-on.
7. **Where the evidence may live.** The record that saw the kill fire must
   be a file under acta, ci, decisions, lessons, opera or petitiones.
8. **Today's data.** Recording the three kills the record already
   describes flags nothing, so no waiver or migration is needed. The
   producer records them in a small PR after this lands.
