## Intent

W-233 part 1 made next issue a step ticket and the ladder's direct writers require it, claim it and copy its line into what they write (`# ticket:` in a spec or build verdict log and in a red log; a `tickets:` entry in the record). The real officina turned it on with `step_tickets: required` on 2026-10-14 (#471). That is a local refusal: it holds an agent that runs the verb, and nothing checks the result. A log written by hand, by a verb run where the setting is absent (W-265's build verdict, written in a worktree branched before the switch, has no ticket), or with a ticket copied from another write reaches master unseen. W-233 named part 2 "the wall": the merge queue refuses a PR whose writes next never ticketed.

After this part, `bisellium check` blocks `ladder.ticket` at a covered log of an open opus that is not justified by its own ticket. The officina check runs in the merge queue's required `officina` job and on a records-only group's `gates` job (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`), so such a log does not reach master. That is the wall, built from the tree rather than the diff, and so without touching `scripts/ci-certify.mjs`.

**What is covered.** A verdict log, `ci/<name>.log` whose leading header has `# phase: spec` or `# phase: build` and one `# opus:` line, and a red log, `ci/reds/<opus>/<name>.log`. Handoff-phase verdicts, gate and certify logs and the record's `tickets:` entries are not covered here (W-233's coverage, less the entries: part 2).

**What justifies a covered log.** Exactly one `# ticket:` line in its leading header, which `parseTicketLine` accepts (the canonical line `ticketLine` writes). The ticket names the log's own opus, action and round: `verdict-spec` with the log's `# round:` for a spec verdict, `verdict-build` with its round for a build verdict, `red` with the log's `# behaviour:` for a red log. It was issued no later than the log's `# at:`, and after the `# at:` of the same opus's previous covered log, since next issues one ticket per write and the writer spends it. This is W-233's `ladder.ticket` for logs, ordering included.

**Which logs are judged: the migration boundary.** History has no tickets, and evidence is never backfilled. So the rule judges a covered log only when all of these hold:
- `bisellium.yml` sets `step_tickets: required`;
- its opus's record is open (greenlit, building, verifying or review); a done, halted or backlog opus is history, and its PR is past the queue;
- its `# at:` is at or after the officina's first ticket: the earliest of the `at` of every canonical ticket line in a covered log and the `# at:` of every covered log that carries a `# ticket:` line. Next writes tickets only when the setting is on, so no log before that moment could carry one. Counting the carrying log's own time means the boundary never passes over a log that carries a ticket line, even when its ticket was issued after it (spec round 2's S1): every such log is judged.

The census of the real officina (`census.out`) shows the boundary is needed and sufficient. Of 1397 `ci/` logs and 127 red directories, every covered log written after the first ticket (2026-10-14T22:48:53.331Z) carries one. W-265's un-ticketed build verdict (13:51 that day) is before it, and its opus is done. Open opera W-093, W-169, W-183, W-208, W-210, W-229 and W-260 hold un-ticketed spec logs from before the switch; they are not judged. Revision 2's boundary is the same instant on master: the earliest ticket-carrying log is later than that ticket (`probes-r3.out`). On master today the rule judges five logs (W-276's spec rounds 1 to 5, `probes-r3.out`) and finds nothing. On the lanes in flight it finds nothing either: W-334's, W-335's and W-335-r2's worktree officinae (`probes-r3.out`).

**A malformed setting blocks.** next holds every opus on a malformed `step_tickets` (W-233). The check now says so at `bisellium.yml` instead of judging nothing.

**Level: block.** The point is the wall: an advise would not stop the queue. Master passes it today (`probes-r3.out`, `gates-proto-r3.out`), so no lane is stopped by history.

Promise ticketed-logs: in an officina with `step_tickets: required` and at least one covered log that carries a `# ticket:` line, `check` blocks `ladder.ticket` at each covered log of an open opus that carries a `# ticket:` line, or whose `# at:` is at or after the first ticket, or is not an exact UTC timestamp, unless its leading header holds exactly one `# ticket:` line that `parseTicketLine` accepts, naming the log's opus, action and round, issued no later than the log and after the opus's previous covered log; one finding per log, the first reason found — limit: judged from the checked tree, so a log of a done, halted or backlog opus, a log before the first ticket that carries no `# ticket:` line, the record's `tickets:` entries and handoff-phase logs are not judged; the first ticket is read from the logs themselves, the earliest of each canonical ticket line's `at` and each ticket-carrying log's `# at:`, so a hand-made ticket or log with an early time moves the boundary earlier (more is judged, never less), and an un-ticketed log whose own `# at:` is back-dated before it is history by its own word; a log that cannot be read through the containment helper is skipped.

Promise off-and-malformed: with `step_tickets` absent, or with no `# ticket:` line yet in any covered log, the rule finds nothing; with `step_tickets` present and not exactly `required`, it blocks once at `bisellium.yml` — limit: dropping the key turns the rule off; `/studio/bisellium.yml` is under CODEOWNERS (W-302), so that drop needs the Patron's review, and no rule judges it (W-233's `ladder.switch`).

Promise never-throws: the rule reads only the checked tree and never throws: an unlistable `ci/`, `ci/reds/` or `opera/`, an unreadable log or record and an unparseable record each read as absent — limit: an unparseable record is the record rules' (`opus.parse`), and its opus is not judged here.

Threat model: mistakes, not adversaries (D-045), as W-233: a ticket line can be forged in a file, and this rule catches records that disagree with each other, not every lie.

### Input domain

The rule blocks; where it judges, a rejection fails closed: an unjudgeable log (no exact time, no or many tickets, an unreadable ticket) blocks. An input outside its judgement (an unreadable file or listing, a log of an opus that is not open, a log before the first ticket) is no finding; the boundary is the promise's limit.
