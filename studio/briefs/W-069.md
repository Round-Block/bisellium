## Intent

**"Available" means the model can complete a turn — never "it appears in a
vendor listing."** The definitional proof case is tokenharbor: a real
models-listing API enumerating 62 models, including paid ones a $0 balance
could not run. A listing is an *offer*, not an *ability*.

This opus builds the thing that turns an offer into evidence: a battery of
minimal real turns, one per candidate **model-and-harness pair**, each landing
as a provenance-carrying entry in `<studio>/models.json`, written by a new
`bisellium probe` verb. **Nothing here renders anything** — W-065's
`GET /api/models`, its resolver and the Seats screen already consume this
record and are untouched. **Nothing here schedules anything** — that is W-071.

The seam is `models.json`'s shape plus `probeBattery(opts)`, both fixed in
*Interfaces*. **Without this opus, W-065's floor is the seated set, every row
`unverified`.** With it, the floor is unchanged and the record makes rows
honest — on the day it lands, through the operator-invoked path alone.

### What this opus is allowed to claim — narrowed, and signed

Revision 1 claimed a probe proves availability "through the same harness
policy `talk` uses". **Sol finding 1 is correct and that claim is withdrawn.**
A real `talk` turn sends the full boot bundle (`talk.ts:355-357`), ~42k
tokens, appended by the claude profile and prepended by the codex one. A probe
sends `systemPrompt: ""`. Omitting the bundle is itself a material trim, and
refusing the cheaper trim flags while doing so was inconsistent.

The claim is narrowed to what a probe actually measures:

> **`available` means: this model, on this harness, completed one minimal turn
> under `talk`'s permission and tool policy, at the recorded time.**
>
> It does **not** prove the model can carry `talk`'s ~42k-token boot bundle.
> A model whose context window or prompt handling fails only at that size will
> be recorded `available` and will still fail a real `talk`. No probe in this
> opus closes that gap.

**Where the line is drawn, and why it is not arbitrary.** Two things differ
between a probe and a real talk, and they are not the same kind of thing:

- the **policy surface** — `--restricted`, `--strict-mcp-config`,
  `--permission-mode dontAsk`, `--tools`, `--allowedTools` for claude;
  `CODEX_POLICY_FLAGS` for codex. This is **per-harness**, identical for every
  model, and a model that chokes on the tool-definition prompt it induces is a
  real and cheap-to-detect failure. **Kept, identically.**
- the **context content** — the boot bundle. This is **per-sella**, not
  per-model: there is no single bundle a model-keyed record could probe
  against, and probing an arbitrary sella's bundle would record one seat's
  answer as every seat's. **Omitted, and the omission is signed above.**

That is the reconciliation Sol's finding 1 demanded: policy surface shared,
context content omitted, the consequence stated rather than discovered.

**Re-open condition, so the limit is falsifiable:** a candidate whose context
window is smaller than the boot bundle, or a recorded `available` model that
then fails a real `talk` on prompt length. Either makes the gap load-bearing
and the fix is a bundle-sized probe in its own opus — priced at the real
$0.42/turn, which is why it is not built speculatively here.

Everything else the record claims is unchanged: `unavailable` means a real
turn failed and carries the vendor's own words; `unverified` means unproven,
for one of four named reasons (never probed, stale, vendor version changed, or
**inconclusive** — see *The control rule*). Nothing routes on `models.json`
and nothing will until the opus that wires dispatch says so.

## What the carve changed, and what it did not

The producer carved at the seam this brief proposed: **`models.json`'s shape
plus `probeBattery(opts)`**. W-071 consumes both and re-specifies neither.

Two things moved, and one thing deliberately did **not**:

- **Moved: the turn cap.** `ProbeBatteryOptions` carries **no `maxTurns`
  field** in this opus, and `runProbe` probes every due pair. A bound on
  automatic spend is only meaningful for an automatic caller, and there is
  none until W-071. W-071 adds the field, enforces it and passes
  `MAX_PROBE_TURNS_PER_RUN` from `tick`.
- **Moved: `defaults.model_probe_stale_days`** and its `check.ts` `DEFAULTS`
  entry. Nothing in this opus reads a staleness threshold: `bisellium probe`
  probes what the operator asked for.
- **Published, not merely used: `readModelsRecord` and `gatherCandidates`.**
  Revision 3 let W-071 claim it consumed a reader and a candidate union that
  this brief never exported, which made the seam a contract W-069 did not
  actually publish (Sol carve findings 1 and 2). Both are now exports of
  `probe.ts` and both are asserted through behaviours 7 and 10.
- **Moved onto the probe: the vendor version.** `HarnessProbe.harnessVersion`
  replaces the top-level block as the trigger's input, because a top-level
  version **strands** capped or stopped pairs permanently — Sol carve
  finding 4, the one finding in this round that was lost work rather than
  wasted spend. See *Why the version lives on the probe*.
- **Did not move: the oldest-evidence-first ordering.** It is not a cadence
  concern. **This opus already stops batteries** — on a failed control, on a
  401, on a usage limit — and a stop plus a deterministic order starves the
  same tail forever, which is precisely the second half of Sol finding 4. The
  ordering is `probeBattery`'s own, lives here, and is asserted by
  **behaviour 4** alongside the stop that makes it necessary. W-071 consumes
  the property; it does not re-specify it.

**The false-green example dissolved, the design did not.** `ui-lead` gained
`harness: codex` on this branch (producer, under D-022's dispatch section,
flagged to the Patron for veto), so `gpt-6-astra` now keys to codex on both
the seat and the listing and the two verdicts agree. The `(model, harness)`
keying, the `probes[]` array, the aggregation rule and the disagreement
warning **stay exactly as revised**: the collision class is real for any
future harness-less seat, and a design that only works because one manifest
row was fixed is not a design. Behaviour 7 and behaviour 9 still assert the
two-harness case, against a **fixture** manifest rather than the live one —
see those behaviours.

### The listing has one implementation, in the wrong package — and a bug

`codexListModels` is module-private at `apps/server/src/http.ts:262`. This
opus needs the same call from `packages/commands`. `apps/server` depends on
`@bisellium/shim` at runtime and on `@bisellium/commands` only as a
devDependency, so the one package both can import is **`@bisellium/shim`** —
also the package that already owns every line of vendor-CLI argv here.

Sol finding 6 found the bug a verbatim move would have frozen. The chain,
verified: the function converts a missing binary, a non-zero exit **and** a
JSON-shape failure into `[]`; `createListingCache` treats any resolved array
as `ok: true`; `mergeModelsWithListing` then downgrades every recorded codex
entry as **withdrawn**, because `ok` is true and the listed set is empty. So
uninstalling `codex` — or codex changing its JSON shape — silently withdraws
every codex model from a shipped screen, which is exactly the record-only
degradation W-065 promised it would not do. The existing server tests never
catch it because they simulate failure by **throwing**
(`server.test.ts:865`), which the real default never does.

**The hoisted function rejects on failure.** That is not a route change: the
already-tested `catch` in `createListingCache` then classifies it `ok: false`,
withdrawal stops firing, and the merge degrades to the record. It is the
smallest edit that makes the shipped default match what the shipped tests
already assert.

/** Each vendor CLI's `--version` line, trimmed, VERBATIM — never parsed,
 *  never compared as a semver. A vendor that is absent, fails or times out

#### The aggregation rule, stated once

`ModelEntry.state` is **optimistic**: `available` if any `probes[]` entry is
`available` (and `harness` names that one); else `unavailable` if any is
`unavailable` (and `harness`/`vendorDiagnostic` name the last such one by
harness id); else `unverified`.

Optimistic, because the dropdown's question is "may this model be seated
somewhere", and if any harness in this studio runs it the answer is yes.

**The signed consequence — an open residual, not a closed one.** The shipped
W-065 reader **deliberately ignores `probes[]`** (`store.ts:146-169`, re-read:
it copies `id`, `state`, `harness` and `vendorDiagnostic` and nothing else).
So a seat whose **own** harness cannot run its model still renders
`available`. Sol's closure answer 3 is adopted verbatim in substance:

> Fixing the live manifest removed the live end-to-end collision **witness**,
> which makes an aggregate false green **easier to overlook**, not less
> likely. Behaviours 7 and 9 keep the fixture from drifting to a single
> harness; they do not close the residual, because the residual is in a
> reader this opus does not own.

Three things follow, all required:

1. a battery whose probes for one id **disagree across harnesses** prints a
   warning line naming the id and each harness's verdict (behaviour 9) — the
   operator-facing witness that replaces the one the manifest fix removed;
2. the disagreement is recorded in `probes[]`, complete, for a consumer that
   does not yet exist;
3. **the follow-on is named, not implied: a screen that renders per-harness
   verdicts** — reading `probes[]` and showing a seat its own harness's row
   rather than the aggregate. That is the opus which closes this, and it is
   neither this one nor W-071. Until it lands, "the aggregate can be a false
   green for a harness-less seat" is a standing, recorded limit of the Seats
   screen.

#### Verdict, from the `Turn` alone

| `Turn` | `HarnessProbe.state` | fields |
|---|---|---|
| `exitCode === 0`, non-empty reply | `available` | `exit: 0, reply: true` |
| `exitCode === 0`, empty reply | `unavailable` | `exit: 0, reply: false`, diagnostic if any |
| `exitCode !== 0` | `unavailable` | `exit`, `reply: false`, diagnostic |
| `exitCode === 3` (`USAGE_LIMIT_EXIT_CODE`) | `unverified` | `note: "usage limit"` — see stop rule |
| harness `available()` false | `unverified` | `note: "harness unavailable"`, no turn |

A turn that produced nothing did not complete a turn. `vendorDiagnostic` comes
from `talk.ts`'s **exported** extractor — it already handles all four
`Turn.raw` shapes, redacts, and bounds to 300 characters, and its header
comment is the specification. The diagnostic is text for a human: no part of
it is ever parsed, matched or branched on.

#### The control rule — a harness cannot condemn without a baseline

Sol finding 7: `available()` checks only `--version`, so an expired token
passes and every model then reads `unavailable`. W-046 §Acceptance rules an
auth failure **inconclusive, not a pass**, and its round-3 review observed a
live expired-token 401. Revision 1 would have written a vendor-wide 401 into
the record as model-level condemnations.

Two structural guards, neither of which parses diagnostic text:

1. **The control.** Each harness's **control** is the lowest-id candidate on
   that harness that is **seated** in the manifest — a model the studio
   asserts it runs. The control is probed **first**. If the control fails,
   nothing on that harness is condemned: every candidate on it is recorded
   `unverified` with `note: "control failed: <harness>"`, no further turn is
   spent on it, and the control's own diagnostic is carried on its row. A
   harness with **no** seated candidate has no control and can never condemn —
   all its rows are `unverified` with `note: "no control"`.
2. **The structured 401 short-circuit.** A claude `Turn` whose `raw` carries
   `api_error_status` in `{401, 403}` stops that harness immediately, same
   `unverified` treatment. That field is envelope metadata, not prose — the
   same distinction `talk.ts:238-242` already draws and blesses. Codex exposes
   no equivalent, which is why guard 1 exists and is the general case.

`exitCode === 3` on any probe stops that harness the same way: the account is
rate-limited, which says nothing about the model.

**Consequence for the targeted path.** `bisellium probe --model X --harness H`
adds `H`'s control to the run when `X` is not itself the control — one extra
turn, so a targeted probe can still honestly return `unavailable`. That is the
price of not writing a 401 into the record as a model verdict.

#### Candidates, and how a pair is formed

The candidate set is the union of two sources, and of nothing else:

1. `codexListModels()` — every `visibility: "list"` slug as `(slug, "codex")`.
2. The manifest: each `sellae` row as `(row.model, harnessForSella(row))` —
   `tick.ts`'s existing helper, imported not copied, defaulting to
   `claude-code`; and each `tiers[].model`, which carries no harness, paired
   with a harness **only** if some source already resolves that id. This
   subsumes the third source W-065 named, because
   `bisellium delegate --model <anything>` writes into `sellae[].model`.

`gen_ai.request.model` is **not** a source (*Survey*).

A tier holder that resolves to no harness yields **no entry and no turn** —
"I do not know which vendor to ask" is an honest answer, and guessing a vendor
from an id prefix is the inference lesson F-1 forbids. W-065's floor still
shows it, `unverified`, which is correct.

#### Order of operations — mark before spend, atomically, per model

Sol finding 3: the shipped reader trusts stored `state` and reads neither
`probe.at` nor `harnessVersions`, so anything not rewritten keeps whatever it
had. A battery that stops therefore leaves a **falsely `available` tail**.

Every battery runs in this order:

1. gather candidates, live versions and the listing;
2. **write the record with every due pair set to `unverified`**, carrying
   `note: "probe due"` and keeping the superseded evidence's `at`, **before
   any turn is spent**. Durable and atomic. From this instant on, an
   interruption, a rate limit, a failed control or a crash can only leave a
   pair `unverified` — never falsely `available`;
3. probe in the order below, **rewriting the whole record after each turn**.
   Each turn stamps its own pair's `probes[].harnessVersion` with the live
   value for that harness — the authoritative version evidence, written only
   here and only by a turn that actually ran;
4. rewrite the top-level `harnessVersions` snapshot for each vendor whose live
   value is **defined**, and `at`. A failed version call **preserves** the
   recorded snapshot value and leaves every unprobed pair's own
   `harnessVersion` untouched (Sol note 12; behaviour 9(iii)).

Step 4 writes a snapshot for a human. The **trigger** W-071 builds reads
`probes[].harnessVersion` from step 3, never this snapshot — see the next
section for why that distinction is load-bearing.

#### Why the version lives on the probe, not on the record

Sol's closure pass found a way for work to be **lost**, not merely re-spent
(carve finding 4). Revision 3 put the vendor version at the top of the record
only, so:

> codex ships a new version → five codex pairs become version-due, all with a
> fresh `at` → a capped or stopped run probes two and skips three → step 4
> records the new codex version → next tick the three skipped pairs are
> neither age-due (their `at` is fresh) nor version-due (the recorded version
> now matches). **They are stranded `unverified` forever.**

That is not the tail rotation this brief promised, and no ordering fixes it,
because the pairs never become due again at all.

**The invariant, and it is the fix:** *the vendor version is evidence about a
probe, so it is recorded on the probe.* `HarnessProbe.harnessVersion` carries
the version observed when **that pair's turn ran**, and is written **only by a
real turn** — the mark-before-spend step in 2 never sets it, because no turn
happened.

Three consequences, all of which fall out rather than needing rules:

- a skipped pair keeps its **old** `harnessVersion`, so it stays version-due
  next tick and the tail drains — exactly the rotation the ordering promises;
- a probed pair gets the **new** one and is not re-spent;
- the trigger is now **per-pair**, symmetric with the age trigger, which was
  already per-pair. This is one fewer concept, not one more.

The two alternatives are recorded so they are not re-proposed. *Advance the
recorded version only when every version-due pair was probed*: one
persistently-failing pair then blocks the version forever, and every tick
re-probes up to the cap — the stranding traded for an unbounded spend loop.
*Zero a stranded pair's `at`*: it makes the record lie about when the pair was
last seen, to induce a trigger that per-pair evidence gives for free.

The record's top-level `harnessVersions` **stays**, because W-065's documented
record shape carries it, and becomes purely a last-observed snapshot for a
human reading the file. **Nothing compares against it.** A builder who
implements W-071's trigger against the top-level block has reintroduced the
stranding bug; W-071's behaviour 2 includes a case where the two disagree,
precisely so that implementation fails.

**Every write is atomic**: serialize, `writeFileSync` to `models.json.tmp` in
the same directory, `renameSync` over `models.json`. A rename within one
filesystem is atomic, so no reader ever observes a partial file and no
interruption can corrupt the record. This is behaviour 6.

Per-turn rewriting means a paid turn is recorded the moment it is paid for:
`tick.ts:121-125`'s idempotence-before-spend discipline applied to the one
artifact that *is* the evidence.

#### Order: oldest evidence first

Never-probed pairs (no `HarnessProbe`, or an unparseable `at`) first, then
ascending `at`, then by `(id, harness)`.

This is a rotation with **no cursor and no new state**: whatever a stopped run
skipped has the oldest evidence next run, so it heads the queue. It lives here
rather than in W-071 because **this opus's own stop rules** — failed control,
401, usage limit — are what create a skipped tail; a deterministic order plus
a stop starves the same tail forever, which is the second half of Sol
finding 4. W-071's cap becomes a second producer of skipped pairs and inherits
the property unchanged.

#### Merge, and what a battery leaves alone

- a probed pair's `HarnessProbe` replaces the previous one on that entry;
- an entry for a model that is **no longer a candidate** is retained verbatim,
  gets zero turns, and ages on its own — a lost entitlement stays visible;
- an **unparseable or absent** `models.json` is not an error: the battery
  starts from an empty record. Advisory data, never instructions;
- an **unparseable `probes[].at`** is treated as **never probed** — so the
  pair is due, gets one turn, and the battery's own write heals it. Revision
  1 had this inverted ("counts as fresh"), which Sol correctly called a
  permanent never-reprobed hole. There is no re-spend loop to fear, because
  the only writer of `at` is this battery, from an injected clock.

## Behaviours to test

Numbered 1–13; **unchanged by the carve**, so a red already recorded against
any of them still names the same slot. Each is its own assertion-level red
(lex §2, check `opus.red_evidence`).

**Reds are assertion-level only if the module loads**, so the builder lands
skeletons first — `probe.ts` and `catalog.ts` with the exported signatures
above and no logic — then records each behaviour's red from its own
single-behaviour run. A module-load failure is one failure, not thirteen.

Conventions: `probe.test.ts` takes the repo path at `argv[2]` and the
behaviour number at `argv[3]` (the `delegate.test.ts` convention).

**No live vendor turn, and no dependence on an installed vendor binary, in
any unit test.** Every behaviour drives a stub profile registry through
`harnesses`, and stub listing/version functions through `listModels` /
`versions`, in the `harness-env.test.ts` manner: the stub records its own
arguments and returns a canned `Turn`.

**Every behaviour carries a positive control** (Sol finding 9): none asserts
only an absence or only an unchanged count. Where the point *is* an absence,
the same run asserts the code path executed.

**Tree-hash ancestry.** Reds are recorded on the work branch descending from
`spec/W-069`, against the tree carrying the skeletons and no implementation. A
red recorded before the skeletons land certifies a tree in which the test
could not run, and is void. W-071 records its own reds against its own tree
and inherits none of these.

1. **One minimal `start` turn per pair; never `resume`; never the bundle.**
   The stub records exactly one `start` per due pair, zero `resume` calls,
   with `model` equal to the pair's id and `systemPrompt` **exactly `""`**.
   Positive control: the recorded `message` is the probe message, so a stub
   that was never called cannot pass.

2. **The verdict comes from the `Turn` alone.** Parameterized: exit 0 +
   non-empty reply → `available`; exit 0 + empty/whitespace reply →
   `unavailable`, `reply: false`; non-zero exit → `unavailable` with `exit`
   equal to the vendor's code and `vendorDiagnostic` from `talk.ts`'s
   **exported** extractor — over all four `Turn.raw` shapes W-046
   established. Asserts the text equals the extractor's own output, so a
   second implementation fails.

3. **A harness cannot condemn without a passing control.** Parameterized:
   (a) the control fails → every candidate on that harness is `unverified`
   with `note` naming the control, **zero further `start` calls on it**, and
   the control's own diagnostic retained; (b) the control passes and a later
   model fails → that model alone is `unavailable`; (c) a harness with no
   seated candidate has no control → every row `unverified`,
   `note: "no control"`, zero turns; (d) the structured short-circuit,
   parameterized over **both** codes the rule names — `raw.api_error_status`
   of **401** and of **403** — each stopping that harness after **one** turn.
   Sol's closure pass found (d) tested 401 alone, so a 403 path broken in
   implementation passed; both are now cases, and a third case pins the
   negative: an `api_error_status` of **500** does **not** short-circuit, so a
   rule written as "any `api_error_status`" fails. Positive control
   throughout: the *other* harness in the same battery is unaffected and its
   rows are written normally.

4. **A usage limit is not a model verdict, and what a stop skips heads the
   next run.** `exitCode === 3` records `unverified` with
   `note: "usage limit"` and no `exit`/`reply`, stops that harness, leaves the
   other harness's probes running to completion, and returns the unreached
   pairs in `skipped`. Then, against the record that run wrote, a second
   battery probes **those pairs first** — the oldest-evidence-first ordering,
   asserted against the stop that makes it necessary rather than claimed in
   prose. Positive control: the pair that *did* complete in run 1 is probed
   last in run 2.

5. **Mark before spend: no interruption can leave a falsely `available`
   tail.** A battery over a record in which every pair is `available` and
   every pair is due: the stub's **first** `start` call asserts that
   `models.json` on disk **already** shows every due pair `unverified` with
   `note: "probe due"`. Then the battery is stopped (control failure) and the
   tail is still `unverified` on disk. Positive control: the first pair's own
   row is `available` again at the end.

6. **Every record write is atomic — tested by interrupting the write, not the
   turn.** Sol's closure pass found revision 3 asserted only that a failing
   *probe* left a parseable file, which any non-atomic writer also satisfies
   between writes. Parameterized: (a) the writer produces `models.json.tmp`
   and renames it, asserted by stubbing `renameSync` and capturing its two
   arguments, not by inspecting the final bytes; (b) **the write itself is
   interrupted** — the stub throws *after* the temp file exists and *before*
   the rename — and `models.json` is then **byte-identical to its pre-write
   content** and still parses. A writer that truncates-in-place fails here and
   passes every other case in this behaviour; (c) a pre-existing
   `models.json.tmp` left by a previous crash is overwritten and does not
   corrupt the run. Positive control for (b): the same battery without the
   injected throw does change `models.json`, so a writer that never writes
   cannot pass.

7. **Candidates are pairs, from two sources, and from nothing else —
   asserted through the exported `gatherCandidates`.** The candidate set
   equals `listing-as-codex-pairs ∪ per-seat (model, harness) pairs`, with
   `harnessForSella`'s `claude-code` default. Called directly, so the
   published carve seam is the tested surface rather than an internal W-071
   cannot reach. A separate case pins `listing: undefined` (the call failed)
   as **not** an empty listing: the manifest-derived pairs come back
   unchanged. Asserted against
   `manifest-harness-collision.yml`, whose harness-less seat holds a
   codex-listed id and therefore yields **both** `(id, codex)` and
   `(id, claude-code)` — the collision class, kept under test after the live
   manifest stopped exhibiting it. A `tiers[].model` resolvable to no harness
   yields **no entry and no turn**. A model named only in the event log is
   **not** a candidate — the drop, pinned.

8. **A listing failure degrades and never empties the record; and the hoisted
   default rejects.** Parameterized: (a) `listModels` rejecting leaves every
   manifest-derived pair probed and every previously-recorded entry intact;
   (b) the real `codexListModels` **rejects** — not resolves `[]` — when the
   binary is absent, when the process exits non-zero, and when stdout is
   unparseable or wrong-shaped, asserted against a stub binary on `PATH`.
   (b) is the guard on Sol finding 6: a resolving-empty default silently
   withdraws every codex model from `/api/models`.

9. **The record's shape, the version evidence, the aggregation, the seam, and
   the disagreement warning.** One battery produces `schema: 1`, an ISO `at`,
   the `harnessVersions` snapshot, and per entry `id` / `state` / `harness?` /
   `vendorDiagnostic?` / `probes[]` sorted by harness, `models[]` **unique by
   id** and sorted. **The version evidence is asserted on the writer's side**,
   three cases, so this opus cannot pass standalone with a destructive writer
   (Sol carve finding 3, and his answer to revision 3's target 7): (i) every
   probe that ran a turn carries `harnessVersion` equal to the injected live
   value; (ii) a row written by the mark-before-spend step, whose turn never
   ran, carries **no** `harnessVersion` — a writer that stamps the marking
   step fails; (iii) with a record seeded with a **defined** prior version and
   an injected live lookup returning **`undefined`**, the resulting
   `ModelsRecord` **preserves** both the prior `probes[].harnessVersion` on
   unprobed rows and the prior top-level snapshot value — a writer that
   overwrites either with `undefined` fails. The optimistic aggregation is
   asserted over all three cases. The written file is then read through the
   **public** `new Store({ studioDir }).api.officina().models` and
   `id`/`state`/`harness`/`vendorDiagnostic` survive on every row — **and no
   id appears twice**, which is what protects `delegation.ts:94`'s
   last-write-wins map. Against the collision fixture, a battery whose probes
   for one id disagree across harnesses prints a warning line naming the id
   and both verdicts.

10. **Retention, corruption and healing — asserted through the exported
    `readModelsRecord`.** Parameterized: (a) an entry for a model that is no
    longer a candidate is retained verbatim with zero turns; (b) an
    unparseable `models.json`, a valid-JSON-wrong-shape one, and one whose
    `models` array holds a malformed entry beside a good one, each yield a
    usable result — `undefined` for the first two, the good entry alone for
    the third — rather than a throw, and a battery over each produces a fresh
    complete record; (c) **a corrupt `at` is classified as never-probed, shown
    by the probe ORDER, not merely by being probed.** Sol's closure pass found
    "it is due and gets a turn" non-discriminating: a run that probes
    everything satisfies it whatever the classification. So the case seeds
    three pairs — one with an unparseable `at`, one with an old `at`, one with
    a recent `at` — and asserts the stub's `start` calls arrive in exactly
    that order. A classifier that treats corrupt as fresh sorts it last and
    fails; one that lets `NaN` fall through a comparator sorts it
    unpredictably and fails. The resulting `at` parses.

11. **`bisellium probe` is a real verb with the house refusal shape.**
    `main.ts` dispatches `probe`; an unknown flag prints the usage line and
    exits 2 **before** any spend (zero `start` calls); `--dry-run` prints the
    candidate pairs and their harnesses, spends zero turns and **writes no
    file**. Positive control: the same studio, run without `--dry-run`,
    writes one.

12. **`--model` requires `--harness`, and the pair is the unit.** `--model`
    alone, and `--harness` alone, each refuse with the usage line and exit 2
    before any spend. A valid pair — including an id in no candidate source —
    probes exactly that pair plus the harness's control, writes only those
    rows, and leaves every other entry byte-identical. An id containing a path
    separator or `..` reaches no filesystem path: the studio path is resolved
    through the shared containment helper (lex §2, check
    `path.escapes.officina`).

13. **A configuration or bookkeeping write grows the event log by zero — with
    positive controls.** Parameterized over two writers against one temp
    studio. First prove each writer *did something*: `probeBattery` returned
    `turns > 0` and wrote a record that parses with at least one `available`
    row; `runDelegate` exited 0, changed `bisellium.yml`'s target value, and
    appended a line to `timeline/patron.jsonl` (`delegate.ts:197`). **Then**
    assert `<studio>/.bisellium/events.jsonl` grew by zero lines. This is
    W-065's carried regression guard — the phantom `workflow.actor_assigned`
    row that brief dropped as spec — and the controls are what keep two no-ops
    from passing it.

## For Sol

Sol keeps the slot; this is not a UI opus, so D-022's `ui-lead` exception does
not apply. Scoped to this half of the carve — the cadence targets are in
`studio/briefs/W-071.md`'s own *For Sol*. Revision 1's seven targets are
superseded; six became spec.

1. **The narrowed claim, again.** `available` now means "completed a minimal
   turn under talk's *policy* surface". Is the policy/context line real, or a
   rationalisation for the cheaper option? Find a failure mode induced by the
   policy flags that the probe *does* catch, and one induced by prompt size
   that it does not — if the first set is empty, the policy profile buys
   nothing and the trim flags should win.

2. **The aggregation's blast radius.** Optimistic aggregation would render a
   model `available` while a harness-less seat holding it cannot run it. The
   live manifest no longer has such a seat, which makes this *easier* to wave
   through — attack that. Is a printed warning plus `probes[]` honest, or is a
   false green on a shipped dropdown never honest? Argue the pessimistic rule,
   or a fourth state, and say what it would cost W-065's untouched reader.

3. **The control rule's own blind spot.** The control is the lowest-id seated
   candidate. What if the control is exactly the model that is broken while
   the rest of the harness is fine? Every row goes `unverified` and the studio
   loses a working harness's evidence until someone notices. Is a single
   control enough, and does the `--model`-adds-the-control rule make a
   targeted probe cost more than it is worth?

4. **Mark-before-spend vs. a crash between step 2 and step 3.** The record now
   says `unverified` for pairs that were `available` a second ago and may be
   `available` still. Does that trade a false-available for a repeated spend
   the next time anything computes what is due? Check the interaction with the
   oldest-first order — and say plainly whether the answer changes once
   W-071's cap exists.

5. **Atomicity claims.** `writeFileSync` + `renameSync` without an `fsync` on
   the directory: on what filesystem does that lose the rename? Is behaviour
   6's assertion (a directory watch, or a stubbed `renameSync`) actually
   implementable hermetically, or have I specified an untestable guard?

6. **The rejecting hoist.** Confirm the only change to the moved function is
   the failure path, that `apps/server/test/server.test.ts` passes unmodified,
   and that no *other* caller depended on the resolve-empty behaviour.

7. **The version-on-the-probe invariant, adversarially.** It closes the
   stranding bug by making version evidence per-pair. Find the interleaving it
   does not cover: a pair probed while the vendor is mid-upgrade, a
   `harnessVersion` written from a version call that succeeded *after* the
   turn rather than before, a record hand-edited to carry a version no vendor
   ever emitted. Does any of them strand a pair or spin a loop? (Revision 3's
   target 7 — "is preserve-on-failure testable inside W-069" — is **answered
   and adopted**: behaviour 9(iii) tests the writer directly.)

8. **The two new exports.** `readModelsRecord` and `gatherCandidates` are now
   public seam, which means W-071 can be built against them *and* so can
   anything else. Is either signature wrong for a second consumer —
   `gatherCandidates` taking `studio` as a path rather than a parsed manifest,
   say, or `readModelsRecord` returning `undefined` for three distinct
   failures a caller might want to tell apart?

9. **The carve seam itself.** `probeBattery`'s signature, the two exports and
   `models.json`'s shape are now the whole contract. Is there a W-071
   behaviour that still cannot be built without changing one of them? If so
   the seam is in the wrong place and should be said now, not after W-069
   merges.
