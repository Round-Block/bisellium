`process.cascade` advises whenever one sella signs both the spec and
review gates of an opus. The QA lex (§1) has the Censor drive the first
hour of every cascade and close the last — so `qa-lead` signing a spec
gate and later reviewing is the lex's *designed* shape, and the rule flags
it.

This collision has already produced one real incident: W-031's spec gate
was re-signed from `qa-lead` to `eng-lead` purely to silence the rule —
backfilled evidence, caught at round 2, now restored. W-030 carries the
advisory honestly for the same reason.

The question: should `process.cascade`

(a) exempt the spec×review pairing when the sella is the QA collegium's
    magister — encoding the lex's design into the rule;
(b) stand as-is, with the QA lex amended so specs are signed by another
    sella (architect or eng-lead), narrowing the Censor's first-hour role;
(c) stand as-is with the advisory accepted as permanent noise on
    Censor-specced opera.

The rule's original target — one *context* building and reviewing its own
work (cascades 9-14) — is not what fires here: the spec-signing and the
review are different agent runs even when the sella id matches. (a) fits
that intent; (b) fits D-014's separation instinct. Not (c).
